# C3 Experiment Integrity Audit

The previous C3 evidence did not close the template-shortcut objection. The canonical 95,479-record table has a template-normalized task/instruction/input/output surface-signature uniqueness rate of 41.0%, the instruction-surface audit contains sampled train--test character-5-gram Jaccard values near 0.99, and the MinHash candidate index is not a full-recall gate. Operation-ticket review and dispatcher-tool routing also remain nearly perfectly predictable with the released character TF--IDF input.

The repair adds a separate 2,880-record compositional control for the two affected tasks. Typed temporal, identity, supervision, thermal, voltage, contingency, and reserve relations determine the labels before language rendering. Twelve wording families are assigned atomically to train, validation, or test, and the split builder does not read labels or model scores. Cross-split character-5-gram similarity is computed over every train--test pair without MinHash or sampling.

All 2,880 normalized surfaces are unique and no normalized surface crosses a split. The maximum exhaustive train--test 5-gram Jaccard is 0.371 for ticket review and 0.375 for tool routing; the corresponding cosine maxima are 0.541 and 0.546. Full-instruction TF--IDF macro-F1 is 0.264 and 0.879. After masking identifiers and numeric values, macro-F1 is 0.335 and 0.354, close to the three-class random reference. The typed relation evaluator reaches macro-F1 1.000 on both sealed test sets.

This audit closes the C3 compositional shortcut-control claim. The corpus-wide 41.0% statistic remains a template-normalized diagnostic and is not evidence of semantic diversity.
